01

Executive summary

ART EYE is a well-built product sitting in a market with an awkward shape: the demand is real and growing, but the willingness to pay in a single city is structurally small. The analysis reaches four conclusions.

The listings business is a commodity with rich incumbents and a near-zero price

Art Almanac (running since 1974), Art Guide Australia, Ocula, Time Out, Broadsheet, Concrete Playground (1.6m monthly users, acquired by Vinyl Group in 2025) and the City of Sydney's own What's On all publish Sydney exhibition listings free. Bloomberg Connects gives away guides to 1,500+ institutions on a philanthropic budget. Nobody wins this on coverage alone, and no consumer pays for it.

The defensible asset is the record and the social graph, not the agenda

What ART EYE has that none of the above has is a personal, cumulative history of what you saw and what you thought of it — Letterboxd's mechanic applied to exhibitions. That asset appreciates. A user with 60 logged shows cannot switch; a user with 60 saved listings can.

Sydney alone caps out around A$120k–250k of annual revenue, executed well

Modelled bottom-up from ~300k engaged gallery-goers and ~60 commercially-motivated venues. That is a strong one-person business and a poor venture. Venture scale requires the social layer taken internationally — the route Letterboxd took to 21m members on a A$19–49/yr subscription.

The economics are unusually forgiving, which changes the recommendation

Infrastructure run-rate is a few thousand dollars a year, and the venue register maintains itself. ART EYE is default-alive. The strategy is therefore not to chase a big number early, but to compound density in one scene cheaply until the social graph is liquid.

Recommendation

Narrow the positioning from “Sydney's exhibition agenda” to the record of what you've seen — the Letterboxd wedge — and win inner-Sydney's contemporary and ARI scene completely before adding a second city.

03

Market definition

ART EYE sits at the intersection of three markets that behave very differently.

MarketCharacterART EYE's relationship
Cultural discovery / what's-on mediaLarge audience, advertising-funded, consumer pays nothing, incumbents are media companiesBuilds here
Personal logging & taste-graph socialSmall ARPU, enormous retention, network effects — the only category here that has produced durable independent businessesMoat is here
Arts-sector SaaS & marketing servicesFew buyers, real budgets, slow sales cyclesMust monetise here

Recognising that these are three different markets — with different customers, price points and metrics — is the central strategic discipline of this document.

04

Demand

The macro picture is favourable

  • 74% of Australians attended at least one live arts event or festival in the year to 2025 — the highest since the survey began in 2009, equivalent to 15.4m people. 98% engage with the arts in some way. [S]
  • Art-gallery and museum attendance sits in the low-to-mid 20% range of people aged 15+. The most recent full ABS release is dated; treat as a floor. [S]
  • Cost is a growing barrier to attendance — the headline finding of the 2025 survey. A free app surfacing free exhibitions runs with that grain, not against it. [S]
  • The Art Gallery of NSW passed 2 million visitors across the expanded campus — the largest concentration of qualified art audience in the country, walkable from most of the register. [S]
  • NSW is committing $1.21bn to arts and culture in 2026–27, including $26.8m for Creative Communities and $29.1m to the 24-Hour Economy Commissioner. [S]
  • The Biennale of Sydney's 25th edition topped Time Out's list of the world's best exhibitions for 2026, across five venues. A once-in-a-cycle demand spike. [S]

The strongest and least-contested job on this list is the second. It is also the only one where the competitive set is nothing.

The behavioural risk

Instagram is not a weak substitute; it is the incumbent behaviour. 91.3% of a surveyed Gen Z cohort use Instagram to search for exhibition information, and 87% of surveyed art buyers used it to find new artists in 2020, up from 48% in 2016 [S]. Any honest analysis concedes that ART EYE's discovery job is adequately served by a free app everyone already has open.

Which is why the record matters

Instagram cannot tell you what you saw in March. It has no state, no history, no rating, no archive. Compete where Instagram is structurally incapable of following — not where it is merely mediocre.

Addressable paying venues in Sydney: ~50–60 [M]. This is the hard ceiling on any venue-subscription line, and it is small enough that the entire market can be sold by one person, by hand, in a quarter — which is both the constraint and the opportunity.

What the incumbents charge

Art Guide Australia and Art Almanac both publish media kits on request only; neither lists public rates [S]. I have deliberately not modelled a specific competitor price. What can be said with confidence:

  • Art Almanac has sold monthly print listings to this exact customer base since 1974, 11 issues a year — a five-decade proof that Australian galleries do pay for exhibition listings. [S]
  • The price point is set by print economics, and the buyer is habituated to paying for a listing rather than for a result.

Implication. Willingness to pay exists and is proven. The differentiator to sell against it is measurability — “37 people saved your show, 12 marked it seen” is something a print listing has never been able to say.

Supply is already solved — an unusual position

Most two-sided marketplaces die on the supply side. ART EYE's is populated and, critically, self-maintaining: weekly validation, monthly discovery, evidence required by database constraint, cost-capped, owner-approval gated [R]. The marginal cost of keeping supply accurate is near zero and does not scale with usage. This is the single most under-valued asset in the product, and the reason a second city is cheap to add later.

Two axes matter: coverage completeness — does it include ARIs and small commercial spaces, or only advertisers and majors? — and personal state — does the app remember you?

ART EYE occupies an empty quadrant. Empty quadrants are either an opportunity or a graveyard; §07 and §09 test which.

The honest conclusion

No configuration of the Australian listings market produces a venture-scale outcome. Venture scale requires the Letterboxd path — the record and the graph, internationally, at 21m-member scale. That is a different, longer, less certain company. It is a legitimate ambition, but it must be chosen deliberately — and it argues for building the record well now, even while revenue comes from campaigns and venues.

Cost base, and the default-alive finding

Supabase, GitHub Pages, Expo and a cost-capped enrichment pipeline put annual infrastructure in the low thousands. Add A$99/yr Apple and A$25 Google Play. With no paid marketing, keeping ART EYE alive costs A$3k–6k a year [R][M]. Break-even needs about six paying venues.

ART EYE cannot fail for financial reasons — only for attention reasons. That fact should drive strategy: optimise for duration and compounding, not early scale.

10

Go-to-market

The bottleneck is social liquidity, not supply

The agenda works for a single user on day one. The feed, trending, friends-who-visited and DMs are worthless until enough people in the same scene are active. A social feature at low density is worse than no social feature — it advertises emptiness. Therefore: density before breadth. Win one scene completely.

Beachhead — the inner-Sydney contemporary and ARI scene

~50k people [M], geographically concentrated in Paddington, Woolloomooloo, Chippendale, Redfern, Marrickville and Leichhardt — all already in the register [R] — socially interconnected, high visit frequency, and structurally under-served by every incumbent because ARIs don't buy advertising. If ART EYE is the only place all 16 ARIs appear alongside AGNSW, that is a reason to install it no competitor can cheaply copy: they would have to list venues that will never pay them.

Channels, ranked by cost per engaged user

  1. Venue-led distribution. 143 venues, each with a mailing list and an Instagram. Give claimed pages away free for 12 months in exchange for one post or newsletter mention. Highest-intent traffic in the market, at zero cash cost.
  2. Opening nights. The scene physically assembles several nights a week. Be there with a QR code — nothing converts a logging app better than logging the show you're standing in.
  3. Art schools. NAS, UNSW A&D, SCA — dense cohorts of 10+/year attenders, annual intake, natural social clustering.
  4. Fair and festival campaigns. Sydney Contemporary and the Biennale bring 25k+ qualified people into a single window [S] — a seeding event and a revenue line simultaneously.
  5. Editorial and SEO. “Exhibitions in Sydney this week” is a durable, compounding, zero-marginal-cost query the register answers better than anyone. Underused today.
  6. Paid social. Last resort. Australian consumer installs at ~A$3–8 each would consume the entire cost base for a few thousand low-intent users.

Platform sequencing and unit economics

Web is live; iOS should follow. The record and the mark-as-seen flow are in-the-moment behaviours that need a home-screen icon and push notifications — already shipped in the codebase [R]. Web-only caps retention structurally.

Blended CAC via channels 1–3 is A$0–2 cash with a high founder-time cost. Direct value per engaged user is about A$0.75/yr — but each engaged user raises the price defensibility of the venue and insight lines. The correct metric is not CAC:LTV. It is engaged users per venue relationship, because that ratio is what venue subscriptions are priced against.

Recommendation & 90-day plan

Strategic statement

ART EYE is the record of Sydney's art scene — the only place that lists every venue including the ones that can't pay, and the only place that remembers what you saw. Lead with the record. Let the agenda be the reason to open it, and the record be the reason to keep it.

Days 1–30 — instrument and sharpen

  • Add analytics for the six metrics below. Nothing here can be judged without them.
  • Rewrite first-run and store copy from “Sydney's exhibition agenda” to the record positioning.
  • Publish T&Cs, privacy policy, moderation policy and an age gate.
  • Add explicit press-image permission to the venue claim terms.
  • Gate social surfaces behind a density threshold, so no user meets an empty feed.

Days 31–60 — supply-side landgrab and first revenue

  • Personally onboard 30 of the ~60 commercially-motivated venues; free claimed page for 12 months in exchange for one mention.
  • Pitch two fair or festival campaigns for the next six months. First invoice is the goal.
  • Draft one grant application against Creative Communities or a City of Sydney audience-development stream.

Days 61–90 — ship iOS and prove retention

  • iOS release with push; drive installs from opening nights and the venue mentions already banked.
  • Ship the free stats and year-in-review surface — what makes the record visible and shareable, and the substance of any future paid tier.
  • Deliver a first aggregate-only insight report to three venues — the artefact renewals will be priced against.

Decision gate at six months

If logging ≥1.5/user/month and W4 retention ≥25%

The record works. Commit to city two and begin building international optionality.

If retention holds but logging is under 0.5

It's a listings utility, not a record. Cut the social stack, keep the agenda, monetise via campaigns, venues and grants, and run it as a profitable small business.

If neither

The free incumbents are sufficient. Preserve the register and the pipeline as the durable asset, and stop investing in the consumer app.

All three are acceptable outcomes. The failure mode to avoid is spending two more years building features without instrumenting which of the three is true.

Method and limitations

Desk research only — no primary interviews with Sydney gallerists, no user survey, and no analytics from the live deployment, none of which appears to be instrumented [R], which is why §13 begins there. Competitor rate cards are quote-only and were not obtained. ABS attendance data is from an older reference period and is used as a floor. Internal figures come from the repository: the venue and exhibition seeds, the pipeline functions, the API surface and the build configuration.

All [M] figures are estimates derived from the stated assumptions and should be rerun as real usage data arrives. The single largest source of error in this document is the absence of any observed retention or logging data for ART EYE itself — §13 exists to remove that uncertainty within 90 days.
